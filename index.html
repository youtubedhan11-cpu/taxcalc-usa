Guides — understand your 2026 tax

Short, plain-English explainers written for this site, using the official 2026 figures.

2026 Federal Tax Brackets Explained

The US federal system has seven marginal rates for 2026: 10%, 12%, 22%, 24%, 32%, 35% and 37%. “Marginal” is the key word. If you are single, the first $12,400 of taxable income is taxed at 10%, the next slice up to $50,400 at 12%, the next up to $105,700 at 22%, and so on. Moving into a higher bracket never reduces your take-home pay — only the dollars above the threshold are taxed at the higher rate. Brackets differ by filing status: married couples filing jointly get roughly double the single thresholds through most bands, with the 37% rate starting above $768,700 of taxable income.

Standard Deduction 2026: How Much Is It?

For 2026 the standard deduction is $16,100 for single filers, $32,200 for married filing jointly, $24,150 for head of household and $16,100 for married filing separately. It is the amount of income the federal government does not tax at all — think of it as a 0% bracket before the 10% bracket begins. Taxpayers aged 65 or older, or blind, add $2,050 (single / head of household) or $1,650 per spouse (joint). Roughly 9 in 10 filers take the standard deduction because it is larger than their itemized deductions would be.

How to Calculate Your Federal Income Tax in 5 Steps

1. Add up your gross income. 2. Subtract pre-tax contributions like 401(k) and HSA to get adjusted gross income. 3. Subtract the standard deduction (or itemized deductions, if larger) to get taxable income. 4. Run taxable income through the brackets slice by slice, as this calculator does. 5. Subtract any tax credits you qualify for — credits reduce tax dollar-for-dollar, unlike deductions. The result is your federal tax before withholding you already paid through your paychecks.

Marginal vs Effective Tax Rate

Your marginal rate is the rate on your last dollar of taxable income — the bracket you land in. Your effective rate is your total federal tax divided by your total income, and it is always lower, because most of your income is taxed at the lower bracket rates and the standard deduction is taxed at 0%. Example: a single filer earning $85,000 in 2026 is in the 22% marginal bracket, but pays an effective rate of about 11.6% on gross income. Use the marginal rate to judge a raise or extra freelance income; use the effective rate to judge your overall tax burden.

Frequently asked questions

What is the standard deduction for 2026?

For tax year 2026, the standard deduction is $16,100 for single filers and married filing separately, $32,200 for married filing jointly, and $24,150 for head of household. Filers aged 65+ or blind add $2,050 (single / head of household) or $1,650 per qualifying spouse (joint). Source: IRS Revenue Procedure 2025-32.

What are the 2026 tax brackets?

Seven rates apply: 10%, 12%, 22%, 24%, 32%, 35% and 37%. For single filers the bands run to $12,400 (10%), $50,400 (12%), $105,700 (22%), $201,775 (24%), $256,225 (32%) and $640,600 (35%), with 37% above that. Joint filers: $24,800, $100,800, $211,400, $403,550, $512,450 and $768,700, with 37% above.

What tax bracket am I in?

Your bracket is determined by your taxable income — gross income minus pre-tax contributions and the standard (or itemized) deduction — and your filing status. Enter your numbers in the calculator above: it shows both your marginal bracket and your (lower) effective rate, plus the tax in each band.

When is the 2026 tax filing deadline?

Returns for tax year 2026 are due on April 15, 2027. If that date falls on a weekend or federal holiday in a given year, the IRS shifts it to the next business day. You can request an automatic extension to file (not to pay) using Form 4868.
